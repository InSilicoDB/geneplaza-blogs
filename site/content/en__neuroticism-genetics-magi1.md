A questionnaire asks whether you often feel tense, whether you worry, whether your mood shifts easily. You tick boxes. Sixty-three thousand other people tick the same boxes.

That is the raw material of personality genetics, and it is what the Genetics of Personality Consortium pooled across **30 cohorts** — 63,661 participants in the discovery analysis, plus an independent replication sample of 9,786 ([de Moor et al., *JAMA Psychiatry*, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2015.0554), PMID: 25993607).

They found one. **rs35855737**, in the gene *MAGI1* on chromosome 3, at p = 9.26 × 10⁻⁹.

Then they tried to replicate it, and it did not replicate. In the independent sample, p = 0.32.

## Why the paper reported that anyway

This is the part worth admiring. The finding survived a meta-analysis across all 30 cohorts at p = 2.38 × 10⁻⁸ — still genome-wide significant — and the authors published the failed replication alongside the hit rather than quietly resting on the combined figure.

That is what a well-behaved result looks like when it is uncertain. Most single-variant findings for personality traits have gone the same way. The honest position is that no individual variant has a large, reliably reproducible effect on neuroticism.

## What did hold up

Two things, and they are the substance of the paper.

**Common genetic variants explain about 15% of the variance in neuroticism.** Not one gene — the aggregate of many common variants across the genome. Real, measurable, and far below the ~40% that twin studies typically report for personality traits.

**Polygenic scores predicted both neuroticism and major depressive disorder** in cohorts not used to build them. The same genetic signal that tracks the personality trait also tracks depression risk, supporting a shared biological basis between the two.

*MAGI1* itself is not a random gene, either — it had previously been associated with bipolar disorder and schizophrenia, which is consistent with the overlap the polygenic analysis found.

## Reading a personality result honestly

Neuroticism is measured by questionnaire. It is a stable, well-validated psychological construct, and it is also a self-report, sensitive to mood on the day, culture, and how a person interprets the questions.

A genetic score for such a trait is a statistical position, several steps removed from anything you would recognise as a personality. It says nothing about whether someone is anxious, resilient, difficult or calm, and it predicts nothing about how any individual will respond to any particular thing that happens to them.
